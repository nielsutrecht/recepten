# Steak bites with mac and cheese

Servings: 4 | Calories: 595kcal | Protein: 58g | Carbs: 59g | Fat: 14g
Section: dinner

## Ingredients


**steak bites**

- 800g lean steak (I used flank
- steak with the excess fat trimmed)
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp black pepper
- Cooking spray (I used avocado oil)

**Mac ‘n’ cheese**

- 280g dry macaroni (boil in salted water till al dente)
- 260ml low fat milk (I used 0.1%)
- 150g light cream cheese (brand:

**Philadelphia lightest)**

- 10g grated Parmesan
- 1/2 chicken stock cube (or 1 tsp chicken stock powder)
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1/4 tsp turmeric (optional for added colour)
- 60-80ml pasta water
- 40g grated low fat cheddar
- 40g grated mozzarella (use skim if you can find it)

**parsley flakes (to garnish)**


## Instructions

1. Boil the macaroni in salted water until al dente, then drain and keep a little pasta water on the side for later. Dice your steak into bite-sized cubes, trimming off any excess fat, then season with salt, smoked paprika, and black pepper. Heat a pan over high heat with a bit of cooking spray and cook the steak bites until they get that deep, golden sear on all sides. Once they’re crispy and cooked through, set them aside. For the sauce, add the low-fat milk, light cream cheese, parmesan, chicken stock, and all the seasonings to a blender cup. Blend until completely smooth, then pour it into a pan over medium heat. Stir for a few minutes until it starts to thicken, then add the cooked pasta along with a splash of pasta water and mix until the sauce coats everything nicely. Finish the mac and cheese by melting in the cheddar and mozzarella until it turns creamy, glossy, and properly cheesy. Spoon the mac into your bowls or meal prep containers and top each portion with a pile of those golden steak bites. Sprinkle some parsley if you like. Store meal preps in the fridge for up to 5 days. To reheat, microwave in a bowl covered with a damp paper towel for 3–4 minutes, giving it a mix halfway through, or warm it gently in a pan.
